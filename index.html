Domestic violence is the harm one partner causes another inside their shared home.

It includes physical attacks, but it also includes fear, financial control, emotional abuse, and humiliation.

Under Ghanaian law, all of these are forms of domestic violence.

Ghana.

Between 36% and 41% of Ghanaian women have experienced spousal violence in their lifetime.

Ghana has a law.

The Domestic Violence Act of 2007 (Act 732) criminalises physical, sexual, economic, and emotional abuse within a relationship.

Ghana has a police unit for it.

The Domestic Violence and Victim Support Unit (DOVVSU) handles about 16,000 cases a year. The country has very few shelters for survivors to go to.

> 50%
of victims never tell anyone

And fewer than 10% ever report the abuse to the police. Most of what happens is not recorded by any system.

A historic community in Accra. We went there to understand, from the people who live there, why so much of this stays hidden.

James Town, Accra

James Town is one of the oldest neighbourhoods in Accra.

It grew around Fort James, a British trading post built in 1673. The community that lives here today is predominantly Ga.

The Lighthouse

The Jamestown Lighthouse marks the coastline.

It stands 28 metres above the neighbourhood and overlooks the fishing harbour and the dense residential compounds behind it.

The Fishing Harbour

Most families depend on fishing.

James Town has an artisanal fishing economy. When catches decline, household income drops sharply, and financial pressure inside homes rises.

The Fish Smokers

Many women in James Town smoke and sell fish for a living.

The income they earn pays for food, rent, and school fees. Despite this, women in the community often have little say in household decisions.

The Compound House

Several generations share a single compound.

Ga families traditionally live in compound houses with shared courtyards. Disputes between couples are usually handled internally by family elders rather than reported to the police.

The Streets

Houses are built close together.

Neighbours often hear what happens inside other homes. Even so, intervening in another family's dispute is widely considered inappropriate.

The Boxing Gyms

James Town is well known for boxing.

The neighbourhood has produced many of Ghana's champions. Teachers we spoke to said children exposed to violence at home often struggle to concentrate in class or stop attending school altogether.

Fieldwork · Ga Mashie

We spoke with the community.

Twelve residents of Ga Mashie and three primary-school teachers. Here is what they told us.

Finding 1

Most residents did not know spousal violence is a crime.

The people we spoke with treat it as a family matter — "shia saji," a house issue — rather than something the law should be involved in.

Finding 2

Only physical violence was seen as abuse.

Withholding money, controlling behaviour, and verbal humiliation were not usually recognised as abuse — even though Ghanaian law treats all of them as domestic violence.

Finding 3

Teachers see the effect on children.

Children from violent homes often arrive at school hungry, cannot focus, and sometimes stop coming altogether. The teachers we spoke with said this is one of the clearest signs of what is happening behind closed doors.
